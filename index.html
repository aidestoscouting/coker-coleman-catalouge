Pyrex Globe Era Cues (ICCC Reference):
  • 1932–1933: Sandblast-etched large Sunshine over Coleman.
  • 1934–1945: Large green straight-line Coleman lettering.
  • 1946–1958: Small green Sunshine over Coleman (front) + PYREX / MADE IN U.S.A. (back).
  • 1958–1962: Curved PYREX over red parallelogram with ® (no lantern icon).
  • 1962–1977: Curved PYREX over red parallelogram with ® and lantern icon.
  • 1977–1983: Parallelogram with "USED ON" model list below.
Coleman Milestones & Suitcase Tank Tabs:
  • 1900–1914: W.C. Coleman starts in Kingfisher, OK ("No Light, No Pay"), moves to Wichita, KS (1902), and launches the 1914 Arc Lantern ("The Sunshine of the Night").
  • 1921–1927: Toronto Canadian plant opens (1921); first suitcase camp stoves debut (1923); Instant-Lite F/A tube arrives (1927).
  • WWII & Golden Era: 520 Pocket Stove & wartime rationed finishes (1942–45); 200A, 220/228, 413/425/426, and 502 Sportster golden era; 1979 400 Peak 1 & 1984 288A.
  • Suitcase Tank Tabs: Bronze/gold tanks (late '40s–'53) → round red tanks ('50s–early '60s) → 2-piece red tanks with tabs inside case slots ('60s–'70s) → tabs widened outside case wall (1980+).

🏕️ Gatherings, Hobby Glossary & Links

Campouts, Conventions & Sunset "Light-Ups":

Through the International Coleman Collectors Club (ICCC) and regional gatherings, collectors meet at campgrounds with tables full of vintage lanterns, stoves, irons, and heaters—swapping parts by day, sharing bench tips around the "Spit & Whittle" table, and lighting dozens or hundreds of appliances at dusk for the classic sunset Light-Up.

Newcomer Glossary:

Fettle: Cleaning, resealing, and tuning a vintage pressure appliance to run safely and brightly.   Birthday Lantern / Stove (BDL): A piece stamped with the exact month and year you (or a family member) were born.   Frankie: A custom mashup of parts from different models.   Money Shot: That first photo of a freshly fettled lantern glowing bright white or blue stove flame rings.   Big Hat / Turd: Collector nicknames for the wide-brim 228 ventilator and the metallic-brown 1970s 275.

🏆 Community & OldColemanParts Tribute

Bob Hitchcock — Where OldColemanParts Began

A longtime Coleman Company veteran who founded OldColemanParts in his 70s, seeding the catalog from his personal collection, wooden dealer service-rack jars, and decades of Wichita repair experience so classic lanterns and stoves could keep earning their keep.

Mike "Cigar Mike" Merz, The CCF Crew & Rob Millican

On January 11, 2011, Cigar Mike carried OCP forward from Bob Hitchcock—spending days hand-counting inventory alongside community friends Murff (Tim Murphy, 1970–2017), Warren, Brent, and Ed—and built The Coleman Collectors Forum (CCF) into a tight-knit workshop brotherhood before passing stewardship in 2022 to Rob Millican in Washington State.

Frank Bebb ("Old Town Coleman"), Dr. Terry Marsh & Sklcolorado

Frank Bebb launched the early Old Town Coleman bulletin board, scanned classic parts manuals, started the "Spit & Whittle Club," and filmed the beloved "Keep 'em Burnin'!" rebuild videos—standing alongside Dr. Terry Marsh's visual archive and Sklcolorado's Peak 1 valve teardowns.

🪓 The 1962 Coleman 501 / 501A Recall

Introduced in 1962 to succeed the 500 Speedmaster, the Model 501 (and 501A) used a circular burner-loop generator with its lighting/control circuit placed where a rich fuel flood could not be positively shut off.

Bob Hitchcock's "Axe & Drill" Recall Account:

Coleman moved quickly to pull 501s out of circulation and replace them with the redesigned 502 Sportster. Bob Hitchcock shared on CCF that returned 501s were rendered unusable at factory/service depots with an axe or by drilling a hole through the bottom of the fount.

The "501-Stamped" 502 Sportster Clue:

Wichita workers reused leftover 501-stamped fount bottoms on safe 502 builds. Always verify the burner top: a straight brass L-generator (502-5891 / R206) crossing over the burner is a safe 502 Sportster; a circular tube loop around the burner is a 501 — display only, never light.
